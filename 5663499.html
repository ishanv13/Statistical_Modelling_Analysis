We can use Variance Inflation Factor scores (VIFs) to get a measure of collinearity within our regression models. Generally, VIF scores of less than 5 don’t warrant any further action. When they are greater than 5 we have to consider whether it is justified keeping all of the predictors in the model. But as the VIF scores of our model’s variables is very less, we do not need to worry about the collinearity within our model.

Conclusion

The analysis of cardiovascular disease (CVD) prevalence across different local authority regions in the UK highlights significant relationships with factors such as poverty, smoking, being overweight, and wellbeing. Our multiple regression models reveal that higher rates of smoking, overweight, and lower wellbeing are positively associated with higher CVD prevalence, while poverty has a negative relationship with CVD, meaning that higher poverty levels correspond to lower CVD rates. The inclusion of an interaction term between poverty and overweight in the second model improved the model fit, demonstrating a complex interplay between these factors in influencing CVD rates. The results emphasize the need for targeted interventions to reduce smoking, manage obesity, and improve wellbeing, while addressing socioeconomic disparities, as these factors collectively contribute to CVD risk. Visualizations further support these findings, with scatter plots showing clear relationships between CVD and the predictors, particularly the inverse relationship between poverty and CVD prevalence.

Conclusion

The analysis shows that customer satisfaction is influenced by several factors, including the socio-economic status (SES) of the store’s location. Stores in medium SES areas tend to have higher customer satisfaction scores compared to low SES stores, and high SES stores also have slightly higher satisfaction scores, though the difference is smaller. Delivery time only affects satisfaction in high SES stores—longer delivery times lead to lower satisfaction. Staff satisfaction plays an important role, with higher staff satisfaction linked to higher customer satisfaction. However, offering a new range of products does not significantly impact satisfaction. By adding an interaction term between delivery time and SES, the model’s accuracy improved slightly, indicating that delivery time affects satisfaction differently depending on the SES of the store’s location. Overall, focusing on faster delivery and improving staff satisfaction, especially in high SES areas, would be effective ways to boost customer satisfaction.
